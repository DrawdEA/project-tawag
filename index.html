The narrative we challenge

Overwork is not a stepping stone.

Many organizations expect new hires to take on responsibilities far beyond their job descriptions without matching pay, recognition, or support. The practice is framed as commitment or experience, but it can normalize burnout before a career has even begun.

Workplace issue

Role creep, low pay, and excessive responsibilities assigned to entry-level employees under the language of experience.

Role Creep Meter

The line between learning and exploitation gets crossed quietly.

New tasks can be meaningful when they come with guidance, time, and support. They become role creep when the responsibility expands but the job title, pay, staffing, and voice of the worker stay frozen.

Learning opportunity

Clear guidance, reasonable scope, and room to grow.

Extra responsibility

New tasks appear, but support is still discussed.

Role creep

The job quietly expands without a formal review.

Exploitation

One starter role carries the weight of several jobs.

What it looks like

When one entry-level role quietly becomes five.

01

Specialized tasks pile up

A junior employee is asked to handle design, analytics, operations, client work, and admin duties as if all of them were part of one starter role.

In the documented account, one starter role quietly absorbed several specialized jobs.

02

Pay stays the same

Extra responsibilities are treated as proof of loyalty instead of a reason to review compensation, staffing, or scope.

The worker earned ₱14,000 a month - well below the ₱21,544 national average (PSA, 2023).

03

Concerns are minimized

New hires who question the setup are framed as weak, entitled, or unwilling to learn.

Pushback was brushed off as failing to treat the job as a "stepping stone."

Research foundation

Fair support keeps people in work.

The campaign connects personal workplace stories to broader labor conditions. When organizations review workload, compensation, and support systems, they reduce the pressure that pushes early-career employees toward burnout and resignation.

₱21,544

Average monthly wage for full-time workers reported by the Philippine Statistics Authority (2023) - far above the ₱14,000 some entry-level workers earn while carrying the work of a seasoned employee.

50-80%

Annual attrition in the Philippine BPO sector, where structural overwork and weak welfare support drive burnout and turnover (Candelario et al., 2024).

96.5%

Call center agents in Metro Cebu and Manila reported headaches, with 93% experiencing insomnia - visible signs of unmanaged workplace strain (Candelario et al., 2024).

Support matters

Tamundong and Caballero (2024) found that perceived organizational support increases job satisfaction and reduces employees' intention to leave.

Narrative voice

The loudest voice is not always the most affected.

Usually heard

Senior voices that defend the system because they survived it: "We went through it too," "This is how you prove yourself," and "That is part of the culture."

Often ignored

Entry-level Filipino workers whose concerns are dismissed as a lack of commitment, even when the problem is unfair scope and unreviewed compensation.

Reframe the story

From "prove yourself" to "protect fair work."

"This is how you prove yourself" hides the cost of unfair work.

Old frame

  • Extra work means dedication.
  • Low pay is the price of experience.
  • Questioning workload is weakness.

New frame

  • Expanded work needs expanded support.
  • Experience should come with dignity.
  • Fair scope helps people stay and grow.

Societal connection

Four goals, one fair workplace.

Workplace exploitation affects more than productivity. It shapes health, decent work, and inequality for entry-level employees who are expected to carry more than their role allows.

3

SDG 3 - Good Health and Well-Being

Excessive workloads and unclear role boundaries contribute to stress, burnout, and poor mental well-being.

5

SDG 5 - Gender Equality

Workplace abuse is gendered: 56% of women in one Philippine study named unreasonable workloads as a primary form of bullying (Armas et al., 2019), leaving female entry-level workers especially exposed.

8

SDG 8 - Decent Work and Economic Growth

Fair compensation, reasonable workloads, and meaningful growth opportunities are essential parts of decent work.

10

SDG 10 - Reduced Inequalities

Role creep reinforces power imbalances between senior employees and junior workers, making it harder for new hires to speak up.

Impact statement

If this workplace narrative continues to be normalized, communities will reinforce systems that undervalue entry-level workers. Our campaign hopes to improve working conditions by encouraging fair workload reviews, better compensation practices, and safer ways for junior employees to raise concerns.

Parallel text

The real story that started this campaign.

Project TAWAG grew out of a documented account, not a hypothetical one - a viral Reddit story surfaced and criticized in a LinkedIn post that shaped how our group understood this issue.

Viral Reddit account

A fresh graduate was paid ₱14,000 a month to build an entire full-stack system from scratch, alone - while also serving as network admin and climbing ladders to fix cabling because the company had no technician.

LinkedIn commentary

The post that surfaced it put it bluntly: "The 'I suffered so you have to suffer too' mentality has to go. That isn't mentorship - that's hazing." It urged readers to stop calling exploitation an "opportunity."

Source: a viral Reddit account and a public LinkedIn post. Quotes are real; names are withheld.
